# Adverbs, postpositions, conjunctions and particles

Source: Peikrishvili, Georgian Morphology.
Rules from Kvachadze (1988) and Chikobava (1946) reflect the literary norm of their time; this file uses neither.

## Adverb endings: თ or დ

- End an adverb derived from the instrumental case in თ, not დ. End one derived from the adverbial case in დ,
  not თ. Example: ✅ `ზემოთ`, `გარეთ` · ✅ `კარგად`, `ცუდად` · ❌ `კარგათ`. (Peikrishvili §179)
- Say and write `ალბათ`, not `ალბად`. The book's footnote explains that this word is an Arabic loan.
  Example: ✅ `ალბათ` · ❌ `ალბად`. (Peikrishvili §179)

## Adverbs: hyphen or one word

- Hyphenate adverbs formed by doubling a stem without და, and antonym adverbs (a pair of opposites).
  Example: ✅ `ნელ-ნელა`, `მთა-მთა` · ✅ `აქა-იქ`, `დილა-საღამოს`. (Peikrishvili §179)
- Write as one word, with no hyphen, adverbs joined by the conjunction და and compound (fused) adverbs.
  Example: ✅ `ხანდახან`, `თანდათან`, `დროდადრო` · ✅ `დიდხანს`, `ცალმხრივ`. (Peikrishvili §179)

## Postpositions: bound or separate

- A postposition either attaches directly to the case form (rarely to the bare stem) or stands as a separate word.
  (Peikrishvili §183)
- Fused postpositions are, in general, one syllable long. The book's one-syllable list is `გან`, `კენ`, `თვის`,
  `ებრ`, and it also treats `ზე` and `ში` as simple. `თანავე` is the exception it names. Example: ✅ `მთაზე`,
  `ლომისებრ`. (Peikrishvili §183)
- Separate postpositions have two or more syllables: `მიერ`, `გამო`, `მიმართ`, `შესახებ`, `გარდა`.
  Example: ✅ `კომისიის მიერ`, `ამის გამო`. (Peikrishvili §183)
- Pronoun and postposition: the postposition, placed after its word, became established in modern Georgian, but a
  few isolated uses of the old preposition pattern survive, all with `ამისა` after the postposition. The book does
  not call either order wrong. Example: ✅ `გარდა ამისა`, `ნაცვლად ამისა`, `მიუხედავად ამისა` · compare
  ✅ `ამის გარდა`, `ამის ნაცვლად`. (Peikrishvili §185)

## Conjunction რამეთუ

- `რამეთუ` has come back to life in modern literary Georgian as a conjunction in the sense of `რადგან`, but it is
  not always used that way: sometimes it serves for purpose or other meanings, which the book calls improper.
  Al. Ghlonti, M. Kekelidze and others established that `რამეთუ` should be used only in the `რადგან` function.
  Example: ✅ `რამეთუ` (= `რადგან`). (Peikrishvili §189)

## Particles

- A particle may be written as a separate word, set off with a hyphen, or joined directly to the word.
  Example: ✅ `ხოლმე`, `თურმე`, `არ`, `ვერ` (separate) · ✅ `მოვდივარ-მეთქი`, `მომიტანე-თქო` (hyphen) ·
  ✅ `ხუთიც`, `რამე`, `სადღა` (fused). (Peikrishvili §190)
- Fixed particles always come before the word (`ნუ`, `არ`, `ვერ`) or always come after it (`მე`, `ვე`, `ღა`,
  `ცა`, `ოდე`). The place of some, such as `თურმე`, is free. (Peikrishvili §190)

### -ო, -თქო, -მეთქი: whose words

- `-მეთქი` repeats what the speaker said or intended earlier. Example: ✅ `ვნახავ-მეთქი`. (Peikrishvili §191)
- `-თქო` is used when the second person is to pass the first person's words on to a third person.
  Example: ✅ `გელოდები-თქო`. (Peikrishvili §191)
- `-ო` shows that the words belong to a third person. The book adds that it occurs mostly in proverbs, although
  its first example is ordinary reported speech. Example: ✅ `დამაგვიანდებაო`. (Peikrishvili §191)
- Set off `-მეთქი` and `-თქო` with a hyphen; write `-ო` joined to the word. (Peikrishvili §191)

### Negative particles

- The book lists four negative particles: `არ` (outright negation, resting on the subject's will), `ვერ` (the
  subject is unable), `ნუ` (forms the negative command) and `როდი` (a negation with a shade of astonishment).
  (Peikrishvili §191)
- Z. Chumburidze observed that `არ`, `ვერ` and `ნუ` combine with `ღა`, `ცა` and other negative words to give three
  groups: plain negation (`არც`, `არაფერი`, `არსად`), negation of possibility (`ვერც`, `ვერაფერი`, `ვერსად`)
  and prohibition (`ნურც`, `ნურაფერი`, `ნურსად`). (Peikrishvili §191)
- The rule that a whole sentence must stay in one group is in `syntax.md` (section 7), not on this book page.

## Interjections and sound words

- An interjection stands apart in the sentence, marked off from the other parts by a comma, a pair of commas or an
  exclamation mark. Example: ✅ `ვიშ, რა ლამაზია!`. (Peikrishvili §195)
- An interjection is sometimes used with the meaning of a noun and can then act as subject, object or modifier.
  (Peikrishvili §195)
- Sound-imitating words for birds, insects, animals and the like, which the book calls functionally close to
  interjections, are printed with a hyphen when repeated. The book does not state this as a rule.
  Example: ✅ `ჭივ-ჭივ`, `ყვა-ყვა`. (Peikrishvili §196)
- The book defines the interjection as an invariable sound, sound group or word, yet it records two cases with
  endings. (Peikrishvili §195, §197)
  - Used in the sentence as a noun, an interjection declines: `ვაი` takes the narrative case (`ვაიმ`) as subject,
    and `ვაი` and `ვუი` take the dative (`ვაის`, `ვუის`) as objects.
  - An interjection of will, when addressed to more than one, is also marked grammatically with the suffix `-თ`.
    This is the book's ნება-სურვილის group of calls, urgings and orders, which is also taken to include oath
    formulas and words of greeting, farewell and thanks. Example: ✅ `არიქათ`, `სუთ`, `გამარჯობათ`.
