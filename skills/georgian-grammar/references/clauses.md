# Georgian Clauses and Reported Speech (რთული წინადადება)

Sources: Kvachadze, Modern Georgian Syntax (1988); Chikobava, General Linguistics (1946).

> [!NOTE]
> Rules from Kvachadze (1988) and Chikobava (1946) reflect the literary norm of their time.

Simple sentences, word order and the basic comma rules are in `syntax.md`. This file covers coordinate clauses,
subordinate clauses, shortening a clause into a simple sentence, and reported speech.

---

## 1. Coordinate Clauses

### მაგრამ Opens the Second Clause
`მაგრამ` is the most widespread contrast conjunction. It always begins the second clause and sets it against the
first. The printed examples put a comma before it. `მაინც` sometimes strengthens the contrast: it usually comes
straight after `მაგრამ` when the second clause drops its subject, and where the second clause names its subject,
`მაინც` comes after that subject (Kvachadze §165):
- ✅ `დაგვიანდა, მაგრამ მაინც მოვიდა`
- ✅ `ყველამ სთხოვა, მაგრამ ნიკა მაინც არ დარჩა`

### Contrastive კი Follows the Stressed Word
Contrastive `კი` is close to `ხოლო` in function, but it never begins the second clause. It comes after the word or
words that carry the logical stress, mostly the subject and less often another clause member. `კი` sometimes appears
in a clause already linked by `და`, `მაგრამ` or `ხოლო`, but the book calls pairing it with a second contrast
conjunction unjustified, because one is enough (Kvachadze §165):
- ✅ `მე დავრჩები, შენ კი წახვალ`

### თუ as "Or" in Alternative Questions
`თუ` is sometimes used as a separating conjunction, and in that use it gives the parts of a coordinate sentence a
question meaning. `თუ` also sometimes joins certain dependent clauses, and then it no longer expresses a question
(Kvachadze §164):
- ✅ `ნიკა წავიდა თუ დარჩა?`
- ✅ `არ ვიცი, მოვა თუ არა`

### The -ც Negatives in Coordinated Clauses May Differ
Negative coordinate sentences repeat the `-ც` particles (`არც`, `ვერც`, `ნურც`, `აღარც`, `ვეღარც`), and the parts
can use different ones, so they need not match (Kvachadze §163):
- ✅ `ვერც ვნახე, აღარც დავურეკე`
- ✅ `არც მე ვეტყვი, ნურც შენ ეტყვი`

### A Dash Stands for a Verb Left Out of the Later Clauses
In a compound sentence whose clauses are joined without a conjunction, a word shared by the clauses is sometimes left
out of the later ones. If the shared word is an independent verb or an auxiliary, its omission in the following
clauses is marked with a dash (Kvachadze §168):
- ✅ `ნიკა წიგნს კითხულობს, ანა — გაზეთს`

### Clause Order and Shared Tense
- The order of clauses in a complex sentence (coordinate or subordinate) is often fairly free, so swapping the
  clauses leaves their relation intact. Often, though, the place of the joined clauses is fixed: swapping them is
  either impossible or gives a different relation between them (Kvachadze §160):
  - ✅ `კარი გაიღო და შემოვიდა`
- Coordinated clauses usually share tense and mood. Alternatives usually share tense and mood and generally the
  screeve; simultaneous events joined by `და` usually share the tense and mostly the screeve; simultaneous events with
  no conjunction share tense and mood as a rule and only rarely differ (Kvachadze §160, §163, §164, §168):
  - ✅ `მოვიდა და დაჯდა`

### A Word After და Can Make the Relation Clearer
Clauses joined by `და` can stand in several relations: simultaneous events, events in sequence, cause and result,
sometimes an explanation of the first clause, and rarely contrast. An extra word in the second clause can make the
relation clearer, but it is optional. For the distance between two events in time the book uses words such as
`მაშინვე` and `მალე`, or a phrase with `შემდეგ`. For cause and result it gives the adverbs `ამიტომ`, `იმიტომ`,
`ამის გამო` and the like, right after `და`. For an explanation, the demonstrative `ეს` stands after `და`. In the
cause-and-result type `და` is sometimes left out; the book's example then has a comma before `ამიტომ`
(Kvachadze §163):
- ✅ `წვიმდა და ამიტომ დავრჩით`
- ✅ `წავიდა და მალე დაბრუნდა`

## 2. Subordinate Clauses (დამოკიდებული წინადადება)

### A Subordinating Conjunction Travels With Its Clause
In a complex sentence the conjunction belongs to the subordinate clause: it opens that clause or sits inside it, so
when the clause changes place the conjunction goes with it. In a compound sentence the conjunction only links the
clauses; it usually stands between them, belongs to neither, and stays put if the clauses swap, a swap allowed only
where the meaning relation between them survives. In a complex sentence the subordinate clause is often set inside the
main one, while in a compound sentence one clause simply follows the other (Kvachadze §192):
- ✅ `ონისემ ერთბაშად ვერ მოიაზრა, რომ ... თოვლში ნადირობა მართლა საშიში იყო`
- ✅ `რომ ... თოვლში ნადირობა მართლა საშიში იყო, ონისემ ერთბაშად ვერ მოიაზრა`

### Some Clauses Must Follow the Main Clause
The order of main and dependent clause is often fixed, meaning the dependent clause cannot change place. A dependent
clause attached with a compound link such as `იმიტომ რომ`, `ისე რომ`, `ასე რომ` is always placed after the main
clause. For clauses joined without a link word the order is usually fixed too: a clause pointing to time stands first
and one pointing to reason stands last. When the main clause is only one word or a tight word group that cannot be
split, the dependent clause may come before or after it (Kvachadze §171):
- ✅ `წავედი, იმიტომ რომ დავიღალე`

### Pointer Word and Link
The parts of a complex sentence often contain a pair of words, one in the main clause (a pointer word) and one in the
dependent clause (the link). This is a common pattern, not a requirement. Often the main clause has no pointer word,
though one can be supplied in thought, and it is also frequent that the main clause has none and none can even be
assumed. When the main clause does have a pointer word, the dependent clause usually has a link too; rarely it has
none, and the pointer alone marks the connection. The pairs the book names, and it adds that there are others
(Kvachadze §169):
- `ისე, რომ` · `ისეთი, რომ` · `იმიტომ, რომ` · `იმისთვის, რომ` · `ისეთი, როგორიც` · `იქ, სადაც` · `მაშინ, როცა`

When a defining dependent clause describes a noun, that noun in the main clause may carry a demonstrative pointer word.
The open-ended list is `ის`, `იმ`, `ისეთი`, `იმდენი`, `იმისთანა`. Some of these are also used with the particle `ვე`
added on the end, written as single words (Kvachadze §174):
- ✅ `ისეთივე` · ✅ `იმდენივე`

### Subject Clauses: ვინც for a Person, რაც for Anything Else
A dependent clause that serves as the subject of the main clause can be joined by a subordinating conjunction (`რომ`,
`თუ`), a relative pronoun (`ვინც`, `რაც`, `რომელიც`, in various cases) or a relative adverb (`სადაც`, `როცა`, and so
on). `რომ`, `ვინც` and `რაც` are the widespread ones; the rest are comparatively rare. `ვინც` is used when the subject
understood for the main clause is a person, and `რაც` when it is something else. When the subject of the main clause
is a determinative pronoun such as `ყველაფერი`, that pronoun cannot be left out (Kvachadze §172):
- ✅ `ყველა, ვინც`
- ✅ `ყველაფერი, რაც`

The pointer pronoun `ის` of the main clause can be restored when it is absent, and can sometimes be left out, so with
the same predicate it is at times present and at times not. This holds whether the clause follows or precedes the main
clause. The book's example `შემცდარია, ვინც გულს იტეხს` can also be said with `ის` added. Two limits: when the subject
clause sits inside the main clause, which is rare, the pointer is always present and opens the sentence; when the
subject of the main clause is a determinative pronoun such as `ყველაფერი`, it cannot be left out, because the word
then understood would be the demonstrative `ის`, which does not mean the same (Kvachadze §172):
- ✅ `ის, ვინც მოვიდა`
- ✅ `ვინც მოვიდა, ...`

### Case of the Relative Word and of the Pointer
- A relative word is a member of the dependent clause as well as its link, so its case is set inside that clause, not
  by the word in the main clause. For `რომელიც` the case comes from the member of the dependent clause it is tied to,
  and its number from the main-clause word it refers to. `რომელიც` is used as a link in every case except the
  adverbial and vocative, most often in the first three. An indirect object is always dative (`ვისაც`, `რასაც`,
  `რომელსაც`). For `რომელიც`, a direct object is dative with a series I verb and nominative as a direct object
  with a series II or III verb (Kvachadze §173, §174):
  - ✅ `ვისაც აქვს` · ✅ `ვინც მოვიდა` · ✅ `ღრუბლებმა, რომლებსაც` · ✅ `ღრუბელმა, რომელსაც`
- When a dependent clause serves as the direct object of the main verb, a demonstrative or determinative pointer in the
  main clause (`იმას`, `ის`) stands in the case a direct object takes with that verb: dative when the main verb is in
  series I, nominative in any screeve of series II or III. The question words `ვინ` and `რა` follow the same split.
  When the dependent clause serves as an indirect object, the pointer is always dative. A pointer for a direct-object
  clause is rare in the main clause, and unless it is determinative (`ყველა`, `ყველაფერი`, `ყოველივე`) it can usually
  be removed, or supplied where it is absent (Kvachadze §173):
  - ✅ `ნუ მომაგონებ იმას, რაც ...` · ✅ `გავიგე ის, რაც ...`
- An object or subject clause that contains a question word without expressing a question is joined to the main clause
  with `თუ`, and this `თუ` is often left out (Kvachadze §173):
  - ✅ `თუ როგორ` · ✅ `თუ რისთვის` · ✅ `თუ რა` · ✅ `ვერ მიმხვდარიყო, რა მიზეზით ...`

### The Particle -ც on a Relative Pronoun
On a relative pronoun in the genitive, the particle `-ც` (`-ცა`) can be left out altogether or moved to the next word:
the noun the pronoun modifies, a free-standing postposition, or an adverb used as a postposition. When the pronoun
modifies a noun in another case form, `-ც` leaves the pronoun and goes on that noun. Such pronouns stay relative and do
not become question words (Kvachadze p. 363):
- ✅ `რომლის ხევსაც` (`-ც` on the noun)
- ✅ `რომლის იქით` (`-ც` dropped)
- ✅ `რომელი წიგნიც` (non-genitive pronoun)

### Do Not Let Another Noun Separate რომელიც From Its Head
`რომელიც` mostly follows its head directly, though other main-clause words sometimes stand between them. With a final
relative clause the gap causes no ambiguity when the word between is the main verb (the head may then be repeated after
the verb), or when the head carries a pointing pronoun. A detached modifier of the head can also stand between. It is
unacceptable for a head with no pointing pronoun to be cut off from `რომელიც` by another noun-form member of the main
clause. The remedy: reorder the words so that the true head touches the pronoun, or rebuild the sentence with the
clause directly after the head (Kvachadze pp. 364-365):
- ✅ `გული ონისესი, რომელსაც`
- ❌ `ონისეს გული, რომელსაც` (the clause refers to `ონისე`, and `გული` stands between)
- ✅ `კურდღელს წააწყდა, რომელიც` (only the verb between)

### Same-Type Clauses: One Conjunction or Repeated
Same-type dependent clauses have the same function, refer to the same member of the main clause (or to all of it) and
share one position: before it, after it, or inside it. Their linking word is usually the same. Three patterns: the
conjunction or relative is used once, with the clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (usually `და`); it is
repeated for the earlier clauses, with the last attached to the one before it by a coordinating conjunction alone; or
it is repeated in every clause, with `და` before the last. A repeated relative may stand in different cases. A
separating conjunction (`ან`) or a contrasting one (the book's example has `ხოლო`) can also join such clauses. The
book shows long chains of same-type relatives as correct grammar (Kvachadze §187):
- ✅ `როცა წვიმდა და ქუხდა, არ გავედით`
- ✅ `როცა წვიმდა, როცა ქუხდა და ქარი ქროდა, არ გავედით`
- ✅ `როცა წვიმდა, როცა ქუხდა და როცა ქარი ქროდა, არ გავედით`

### Place Clauses
A place clause is joined by the conjunction `რომ` or by a relative adverb. `სადაც` (variants `სადაც კი`, `საცა`,
`სად`) points to the place where something is or happens, `საითაც` and `საითკენაც` show the direction of movement, and
`საიდანაც` the starting point. The main clause usually has a pointer adverb (`იქ`, `იქვე`, `იქით`, `იქითკენ`,
`იქიდან`, `ყველგან`). Leaving the pointer out happens rarely, and mainly when the subordinate clause comes first. Some
adverbs such as `ყველგან` are always present and cannot be left out. A `რომ` place clause usually opens the sentence
and rarely sits inside the main clause; a clause with a relative adverb mostly comes first or last (Kvachadze §176):
- ✅ `იქ, სადაც` · ✅ `იქით, საითკენაც` · ✅ `ყველგან, სადაც`

### Time Clauses
Pick the time conjunction by how the two actions relate. Time clauses usually stand first or last and are rare inside
the main clause (Kvachadze §177):
- Two long, unbroken actions that fully overlap usually take the imperfect, with `რომ`, `როდესაც`, `როცა`, `სანამ`.
  Simultaneity is usually expressed only in the past (Shanidze, quoted by the book).
- Actions repeated at the same time take the same verb form (imperfect, ხოლმეობითი), sometimes different ones, with
  `როდესაც`, `როცა`, `რომ`.
- When the subordinate action comes first, the clause usually opens the sentence and both parts are mostly aorist.
- Immediate sequence is shown with `რაწამს` or `როგორც კი` at the start of the clause, or `თუ არა` after its verb, and
  the main clause may have `მაშინვე`.
- `რაც` marks the point from which the main action starts; that clause usually comes last, the main clause may have
  `მას შემდეგ` or `მას აქეთ`, and `რაკი` rarely does the same job.
- Where the main action comes first: `რომ`, `როცა`, `ვიდრე`, `სანამ`. With `სანამ` and `ვიდრე` the clause opens the
  sentence in the book's main examples and rarely stands mid-sentence or last, and its verb is mostly ხოლმეობითი with
  an aorist main verb, though other combinations occur. The main clause may carry a pointer such as `მანამ`, `მაშინ`,
  `მაშინვე`.
- ✅ `რაწამს` · ✅ `როგორც კი` · ✅ `მას შემდეგ, რაც`

### Manner and Degree Pairs With რომ
A manner clause with `რომ` is tied to a pointer adverb in the main clause, such as `ისე`, `ასე`, `ისეთნაირად` or
`იმგვარად`, or the degree adverb `იმდენად`; the pointer is rarely left out. The clause mostly comes last and spells
out what the pointer means. With `ისე` it often also conveys a kind of result, and with `იმდენად` it gives the degree
and a kind of result. `რომ` itself is sometimes left out, and the pointer then signals the link. If the clause is moved
in front of the main clause, which is rare, `რომ` often drops, though it can stay when the clause verb is II
კავშირებითი or II თურმეობითი. Only rarely does the pointer attach to the clause to form the complex conjunction
`ისე რომ` (`ასე რომ`) (Kvachadze §178):
- ✅ `ისე ... რომ` · ✅ `იმდენად ... რომ` · ✅ `... , ისე რომ ...`

### Proportional Pairs
Measure and proportion clauses use pairs such as `რამდენადაც ... იმდენად`, `რამდენჯერაც ... იმდენჯერ`, and
`რაც (უფრო) ... უფრო / მით უფრო / იმდენად (უფრო)`. The subordinate clause generally comes first; with the `რაც` type
it only rarely follows the main clause. With the `რაც` type both parts usually have the same verb form, normally the
imperfect, and with `რამდენჯერაც` the same form is also usual. In the other measure clauses the two forms may be the
same or different. The book lists as departures from the established literary rule the nominative `რამდენიც ...
იმდენი` and `რამდენიც ... იმდენად` (Kvachadze §178):
- ✅ `რაც უფრო ... მით უფრო` · ✅ `რამდენჯერაც ... იმდენჯერ` · ✅ `რამდენადაც ... იმდენად`
- ❌ `რამდენიც ... იმდენი`

### Cause Clauses
Cause clauses are joined by `რომ`, `იმიტომ რომ`, `რადგანაც`, `ვინაიდან`, `რაკი`, `რახან` and `რაღაი` (Kvachadze §179):
- A `რომ` clause usually comes last, and the main clause almost always has `მიტომ`, `იმიტომ` or `იმის გამო`. When
  `იმიტომ` (`ამიტომ`) lands at the end of the main clause, it sometimes joins the clause by intonation to form
  `იმიტომ რომ` (`ამიტომ რომ`), with the comma before it. A `რომ` clause placed first takes `ამიტომ` or `ამის გამო`
  in the main clause.
- `რადგანაც` and `ვინაიდან` clauses mostly follow the main clause, which then has no pointer; placed first they may
  take `ამიტომ` or `ამის გამო`, and they rarely sit inside the main clause.
- `რახან` and `რაღაი` clauses usually come first.
- `რაკი` is also a time word; as a cause word its clause usually comes first and may take `ამიტომ` or `ამის გამო`.
- With no pointer the cause link is weaker and the clause more often reads as time or condition-cause.
- ✅ `იმიტომ რომ` · ✅ `ვინაიდან ... , ამიტომ` · ✅ `რაკი ... , ამიტომ`

### Cause Pointers Differ From Purpose Pointers
In modern Georgian the pointers `იმიტომ`, `ამიტომ`, `იმის გამო`, `ამის გამო` belong to cause, and `იმისთვის`,
`ამისთვის`, `მისთვის` belong to purpose. The main clause must use the pointer of its own kind; the book calls swapping
them inadmissible under the modern literary norm. It queries `ამისათვის` in the main clause of a cause sentence and
`იმიტომ` in the main clause of a purpose sentence. Pointers are rare in purpose sentences in the first place, and the
book gives `იმისთვის` and `მისთვის` as the possible ones (Kvachadze §180):
- ✅ `იმიტომ რომ` (cause) · ✅ `იმისთვის რომ` (purpose)
- ❌ `რადგანაც ... , ამისათვის` · ❌ `იმიტომ ... რომ` (where the clause states a purpose)

### Verb Form in Purpose Clauses
In a purpose sentence the main clause is usually indicative and rarely subjunctive, and the two clauses usually have
different verb forms. Four patterns (Kvachadze §180):
- Present main clause, goal still to be met: optative (II კავშირებითი) in the clause; a present main clause may also
  take the present subjunctive (I კავშირებითი).
- Past indicative main clause (usually aorist, less often imperfect, ხოლმეობითი or თურმეობითი) with a goal that had to
  be met in the past: II თურმეობითი in the clause, in the sense of III კავშირებითი.
- Aorist main clause whose action precedes the moment of speaking, with a goal to be met after that moment:
  II კავშირებითი.
- Main clause with `უნდა` plus II კავშირებითი: II კავშირებითი in both clauses, with the main action in the future.
- ✅ `სცემენ, რომ გამოამტვრიონ` (present main clause, optative) · ✅ `მისთვის მინდა, რომ მსახურებდეს` (present
  subjunctive) · ✅ `მივხურე, რომ არ შეჰყოლოდნენ` (past goal)

### Result Clauses
A result clause has a fixed place: normally the main clause comes first and the result clause second, and it can only
very rarely sit inside the main clause. It is joined by `ასე რომ`, `რის გამოც` or `რის შედეგადაც`. A clause with
`ისე რომ` can read as manner or as result; where showing the result is the main point, the book counts it as more a
result clause than a manner clause. A clause joined with `რის შემდეგაც` must be told apart from a result clause: it
gives what happens after the main action is completed, a sequence, not a result, and it always stands last
(Kvachadze §181):
- ✅ `რის გამოც` (result) · ✅ `რის შემდეგაც` (sequence)

### თორემ Opens the Second Clause
`თორემ` is a conditional-consequence link which the book, citing Shanidze, derives from `თუ` and the particle `არა`,
roughly "if not". It begins a dependent clause that always stands last, after the main clause. The clause shows what
will follow if the action named in the main clause is not carried out. `თორემ` sometimes simply sets one clause against
another (Kvachadze §182):
- ✅ `იჩქარე, თორემ ვერ მოასწრებ`

### Concessive Clauses: Two Groups, Two Moods
Group one states an obstacle that really exists, and the indicative is characteristic of it; its conjunctions are
`თუმცა` (also `თუმც`, `თუმცაღა`) and the whole phrase `მიუხედავად იმისა, რომ`. Group two states a possible, supposed
obstacle that still cannot stop the action, and the subjunctive is characteristic of it: the verb is in the I or II
subjunctive, or in the II თურმეობითი in place of the III subjunctive. Its conjunctions are `რომ` (helped by the
particle `-ც` in the dependent clause), `გინდ`, `თუნდ` (`თუნდა`, `თუნდაც`) and relative words with `უნდა`, where
`გინდ` sometimes replaces `უნდა`. The book's general list of concessive conjunctions also has `გინდაც`, `რაგინდ` and
`თუგინდ`. `თუნდაც` sometimes comes with `რომ` (`თუნდაც რომ`), but `თუნდაც` alone is enough (Kvachadze §183):
- ✅ `თუმცა დაიღალა, მაგრამ გააგრძელა`
- ✅ `თუნდ დაიღალოს, გააგრძელებს`

### ოღონდ: "Provided That"
`ოღონდ` (also `ოღონც`, `ოღონდაც`, `ოღონდ კი`) joins to the main clause a dependent clause of conditional-concessive
meaning. This type approaches a compound sentence with a contrasting conjunction and can be called an intermediate
type. Sometimes `ოღონდ` (`ოღონდ კი`) stands in the main clause with a somewhat contrasting meaning, and the dependent
clause is then joined by `თუნდ` (`თუნდა`) or `თუ გინდ`. `ოღონდაც` sometimes acts as a one-word sentence (a main
clause), set off by a comma before `რომ`. The book states no verb-form rule (Kvachadze §183):
- ✅ `წავა, ოღონდ ადრე დაბრუნდეს`
- ✅ `თუნდ დავიღალო, ოღონდ კი ვნახო`

### მართალია Marks a Concession
The inserted word `მართალია` gives the first part of a complex sentence concessive content, and the following clause
(the main one) then contains the contrasting `მაგრამ`. It is set off by a comma when it stands first and by two commas
when it stands inside the clause. It can be replaced by `თუმცა` without changing the content, but `თუმცა` is the main
concessive conjunction while `მართალია` is only inserted (Kvachadze §183):
- ✅ `მართალია, გვიანია, მაგრამ მოვასწრებთ`
- ✅ `ის, მართალია, გვიან მოვიდა, მაგრამ მოასწრო`

### რაც as a Comment on the Whole Main Clause
A dependent clause introduced by the relative `რაც` (in various case forms) refers to the whole main clause and gives
the speaker's conclusion, evaluation or remark about what the main clause says. It always follows the main clause.
Nothing in the main clause announces it; only the intonation shows that the sentence is not finished. If `რაც` is
replaced by the demonstrative `ეს` in the same case, the sentence becomes a compound one joined with `და`. Without
`და` the clause becomes an independent one, set off from the preceding clause by a semicolon (Kvachadze §184):
- ✅ `ის გვიან მოვიდა, რამაც გაგვაბრაზა`
- ✅ `ის გვიან მოვიდა და ამან გაგვაბრაზა`
- ✅ `ის გვიან მოვიდა; ამან გაგვაბრაზა`
- forms: `რამაც` / `რითაც`

### Punctuation of Clauses With No Conjunction
Several kinds of dependent clause are joined without a conjunction (Kvachadze §186):
- An object or subject clause always follows the main clause. After the main clause a comma is usually written, rarely
  a colon or dash. This covers clauses with a question word (where `თუ` could be restored) and other clauses (where
  `რომ` could be restored).
- A time clause comes before the main clause and is separated from it by a dash.
- A cause clause follows the main clause and is separated by a colon.
- A conditional clause always stands first and is separated from the main clause by a dash.
- A second part that explains the first in detail is also set off by a colon in the book's examples.
- ✅ `ვიცი, მოვა` · ✅ `დაგირეკავს — უპასუხე` · ✅ `ვერ მოვა: ავად არის`

## 3. Shortening a Clause Into a Simple Sentence

### Shortening Is a Style Choice
A complex sentence and its simple counterpart are not equal in style. The clause is the richer means of saying a thing
fully and exactly, yet the simple sentence is often the more flexible one; in each case prefer whichever says the
content more clearly, exactly and naturally. So the nominal style in `syntax.md` §4 is one legitimate option, not
always a fault. A complex sentence cannot always be shortened. Shortening is usually possible only when the clause does
the work of a sentence member (subject, object, attribute, adverbial), rarely otherwise, and in general only when its
verb readily gives a masdar or participle. The `ისეთი ... რომ` example, an attributive clause, cannot become an
attribute, so that sentence has no simple form. A result clause is rarely replaced, and then only nominally: in the
`რის გამოც` example the masdar plus `გამო` is made from the main verb, so the simple form really matches a cause
clause. The reverse also often fails: a simple sentence may have no complex form (Kvachadze §190, §191):
- ✅ keep `ისეთი ბნელი ღამე იყო, რომ ...`

### Object Clause With რომ
`რომ` and any pointing word drop, the clause verb becomes a masdar (a participle after `ვინც` or `რაც`), and that word
fills the object slot of the main verb. A compound predicate loses its copula, and its noun part either stays or
becomes an abstract noun (`გულწრფელია` → `გულწრფელობაში`). A noun subject goes into the genitive: plain after an
intransitive verb (`ავადმყოფის ... განკურნებაში`), with `მიერ` in the book's transitive example
(`ვანოს მიერ ... დათვის მოკვლა`), where the object turns genitive too. A 1st or 2nd person pronoun, and sometimes a
3rd person one, is replaced by the matching possessive (`ჩემი`, `ჩვენი`, `შენი`, `თქვენი`, `თავისი`; the examples also
use `მისი`, `მათი`). Shanidze, quoted by the book: if the clause verb is in the objective version, the matching
possessive is required (`შვილი ... დამიბრუნდა` → `ჩემი შვილის ... დაბრუნება`) (Kvachadze §191):
- ✅ `ბავშვებმა ვერ გაიგეს, რომ შენ სანადიროდ წახვედი` → `ბავშვებმა ვერ გაიგეს შენი სანადიროდ წასვლა`

### Subject Clause With ვინც ... ის
The pointing word, if there is one (such as `ის`), and the conjunction or relative word drop. The clause verb becomes a
masdar or a participle of the matching voice and serves as subject of the main verb, and the other words of the clause
stay attached to it. After `ვინც` and `რაც` the examples give a participle, and an object such as `ეს` turns genitive
(`ამის`). A compound predicate loses its copula, and its noun part either stays as it is (`ვაჟკაცი`) or becomes an
abstract noun (`შენ რომ ასეთი გულადი ხარ` → `შენი ასეთი გულადობა`) (Kvachadze §190, §191):
- ✅ `ვინც მოითმენს, ის მოიგებს` → `მომთმენი მოიგებს`
- ✅ `ვინც ეს დაწერა, გენიოსია` → `ამის დამწერი გენიოსია`
- ✅ `რაც დაიკარგა, აღარ დაგვიბრუნდება` → `დაკარგული აღარ დაგვიბრუნდება`
- ✅ `ვინც ვაჟკაცია, წამოვიდეს` → `ვაჟკაცი წამოვიდეს`

### Relative Clause With რომელიც or რომ
An attributive clause can usually be shortened only when a relative pronoun or `რომ` joins it. The joining word and any
pointing word drop, and the clause verb becomes a participle attached as an attribute to the noun it explained. As
subject of an active-voice verb, the relative pronoun gives an active participle; as direct object, or as subject of a
passive-voice verb, it gives a passive participle. If the predicate is the copula `არის` (or a one-person substitute)
with an adjective or participle, the copula drops and that word becomes the attribute. A compound series III form
keeps its participle unchanged. If the predicate is `აქვს` plus a passive participle, `აქვს` drops and the clause
subject joins the participle as a bare stem (`ჟანგმოკიდებული`); a clause subject or direct object can also join in
this way a passive participle made from a simple verb (`ფრთებშეკუმშული`) (Kvachadze §191):
- ✅ `აი აქ არის ის კაცი, ვინც ეს ამბავი თქვა` → `აი აქ არის ამ ამბის მთქმელი კაცი`
- ✅ `ბავშვი, რომელიც ბეჯითია, ყველას უყვარს` → `ბეჯითი ბავშვი ყველას უყვარს`
- ✅ `ხარ-ირემს, რომელიც ამ დილით მონადირეებს მოეკლათ` → `მონადირეებისაგან ამ დილით მოკლულ ხარ-ირემს`
- ✅ `ეკალში ვარდი შევნიშნე, ობლად რომ ამოსულიყო` → `ეკალში ობლად ამოსული ვარდი შევნიშნე`

When an attributive clause runs to many words and the noun it explains in the main clause already has an attribute, it
seems more natural to turn the clause into a detached (set-off) attribute. This is stated for that double condition
only; it is not a general punctuation rule. The reverse also works: an ordinary or detached attribute can be expanded
into an attributive clause (Kvachadze §191):
- ✅ `ქალაქში მიმავალი პეტრე, რომელიც გაკვირვებული იყო უცნობის ვინაობით, ხმას არ იღებდა` → `ქალაქში მიმავალი პეტრე, უცნობის ვინაობით გაკვირვებული, ხმას არ იღებდა`

### Time Clause
The clause verb becomes a masdar, and the joining word and correlative drop as a rule. The form depends on how the two
actions relate in time (Kvachadze §191):
- Simultaneous: `-ისას` or `-ის დროს`.
- Main action first (`სანამ`, `ვიდრე`): adverbial case plus `-მდე` or `-მდის`.
- Main action directly after the clause action (`როგორც კი`, `თუ არა`, `რაწამს`): `-ისთანავე`.
- Main action later: `-ის შემდეგ`.
- A subject or object not shared with the main clause goes into the genitive before the masdar. A word shared by both
  clauses stays as a plain subject or object of the main verb. With a compound predicate the noun part usually takes
  the change (`როცა პატარა ვიყავი` → `პატარაობისას`). Rarely, the clause becomes a detached time phrase set off by
  commas.
- ✅ `სოფელში რომ შევდიოდით, ძაღლები ყეფდნენ` → `სოფელში შესვლისას ძაღლები ყეფდნენ`
- ✅ `სანამ პატრონი მოვიდოდა, ...` → `პატრონის მოსვლამდე ...`
- ✅ `როგორც კი ხალხი ახმაურდა, იგი ფეხზე წამოხტა` → `ხალხის ახმაურებისთანავე იგი ფეხზე წამოხტა`
- ✅ `პირი რომ დაიბანა ნუშომ, ჩაიდანს მოუჯდა` → `პირის დაბანის შემდეგ ნუშო ჩაიდანს მოუჯდა`

### Place Clause
The verb of a place clause usually becomes a masdar in the genitive plus `ადგილას`, or `ადგილიდან`, `ადგილისაკენ`,
`ადგილამდე`; the whole phrase then serves as a place adverbial of the main verb. The ending follows the direction,
and `სადაც` with the matching `იქ`, `იქიდან`, `იქითკენ`, `იქამდე` of the main clause drops (Kvachadze §191):
- ✅ `სადაც დაიბადა, იქიდან ჩამოვიდა` → `დაბადების ადგილიდან ჩამოვიდა`
- ✅ `სადაც ცხვარი დაეკლათ, მწყემსმა იქამდე ძლივს მიაღწია` → `მწყემსმა ცხვრის დაკვლის ადგილამდე ძლივს მიაღწია`

### Manner and Comparison Clause
A manner clause can usually be shortened only when it expresses likeness or comparison. The joining word and
correlative drop, and the book gives three basic patterns (Kvachadze §191):
- (a) The verb becomes a masdar in the genitive plus the postposition `-ებრ` or `-მებრ` (`რჩევისამებრ`).
- (b) The verb becomes a participle in the adverbial case (`-ად`); a negative predicate gives a negative participle
  (`დაუნახავად`).
- (c) If the clause predicate repeats the form of the main verb or is left out, the compared word takes `-ვით` on its
  nominative or dative form (`მასავით`, `ბოლოსავით`, `ქალებივით`, `რომანივით`), and a shared word left out of the
  clause is restored (`იოთამეს სახლივით`).
- `-ვით` forms can be swapped for `-ებრ` forms (`მისებრ`), but the `-ვით` ones seem more natural to the author, and
  other shortenings sometimes occur.
- ✅ `მონადირე სწორედ ისე მოიქცა, როგორც ურჩიეს` → `მონადირე სწორედ [მათი] რჩევისამებრ მოიქცა`
- ✅ `თედემ სამი წელი დაჰყო ისე, რომ კაცის სახე არ დაუნახავს` → `თედემ სამი წელი დაჰყო კაცის სახის დაუნახავად`
- ✅ `როგორც ის ცხოვრობს, ისე ვიცხოვრებ` → `მასავით ვიცხოვრებ`
- ✅ `მისი ცხოვრება ისე წაუკითხეს, როგორც კარგი რომანი` → `მისი ცხოვრება კარგი რომანივით წაუკითხეს`

### Cause Clause
The verb of a cause clause (`იმიტომ, რომ`, `რადგანაც`, `რაკი`) becomes a masdar in the genitive followed by `გამო`,
serving as a cause adverbial of the main verb. The conjunction and the correlative word (`იმიტომ`) are dropped
(Kvachadze §191):
- ✅ `რადგანაც გაწვიმდა, სასეირნოდ ვეღარ წავედით` → `გაწვიმების გამო სასეირნოდ ვეღარ წავედით`
- ✅ `რაკი ბინდი ახლოვდებოდა, ...` → `ბინდის მოახლოების გამო ...`

### Condition Clause
Condition clauses have no clear matching sentence member, so a simple sentence can only sometimes, and only
approximately, carry their content. Where it does, the verb becomes a masdar in the genitive followed by
`შემთხვევაში`, and the conjunction (and any correlative word) drops. If the clause predicate is negative (has `არ`),
the verb becomes a noun in `-ობა` formed from the negative participle, in the genitive, again followed by
`შემთხვევაში` (Kvachadze §191):
- ✅ `თუ მტერს ის გორაკი დაუთმეს, ...` → `მტრისათვის იმ გორაკის დათმობის შემთხვევაში ...`
- ✅ `ჩხუბი რომ არ მოსვლოდათ, ...` → `ჩხუბის მოუსვლელობის შემთხვევაში ...`

## 4. Reported Speech (ირიბი ნათქვამი)

The basic reported-speech rules (direct quotes, `-ო`, no tense back-shift) are in `syntax.md` §2.

### Statements and Commands Become Indirect Speech With რომ
Indirect speech gives the sense of someone's words from the author's own viewpoint, not word for word. When the direct
speech is a statement or a command, the matching indirect speech is joined to the author's words (the main clause) by
`რომ`, and the verb's person changes to fit the new viewpoint (`მე არა ვარ` → `ის არ არის`). No verb-form rule is set
for commands: after a past author's verb, two of the three examples use the pluperfect form (`შეგვერეკა`) and one the
optative (`გავიდეს`). Indirect speech is also sometimes given by a masdar instead of a clause (Kvachadze §193):
- ✅ `მე არა ვარ მტაცებელი ფრინველი` → `რომ ის არ არის მტაცებელი ფრინველი`
- ✅ `გადი გარეთ` → `უბრძანა, რომ გარეთ გავიდეს`
- ✅ `კამეჩები შერეკეთ` → `გვიბრძანა, რომ კამეჩები შეგვერეკა`

### Address Forms, Interjections, Modal Words and Particles
Direct speech often contains forms of address, interjections, modal words and particles, which the matching indirect
speech either does not carry over or gives a different role. Indirect speech is colourless and inexact compared with
direct speech. Turning direct speech into indirect is not always possible (and is not always wanted); where both are
possible, one of the two is chosen (Kvachadze §193).

### The Particle -ო
`-ო` marks speech repeated from a third person (or words showing their intention). It is often added to proverbs, whose
speaker is understood as a third person, and Shanidze, quoted by the book, calls it usual with `თითქოს` or `თითქო`. It
is also used when the first-person verb is plural: with `ვამბობდით` the book has `-ო`, and putting that verb in the
singular swaps it for `-მეთქი`. `-ო` joins the speech directly, with no hyphen. A simple sentence has it once, while
a merged or complex sentence may have it several times; it usually joins the verb-predicate, rarely another member; and
the word carrying it mostly stands at the end of the sentence (Kvachadze §194):
- ✅ `ეხლა გაიგონონო, — ამბობდა ხოლმე`
- ✅ `მჩხავანა კატა თაგვს ვერ დაიჭერსო`
- ✅ `როგორ რუხრუხებსო! — ვამბობდით ჩვენ`

### -მეთქი and -თქო Take a Hyphen
`-მეთქი` marks the repeating of first-person speech (or of what the speaker thought or intended), usually unchanged,
though the words can also come back with some change. When the first-person verb is plural, `-ო` is used instead.
`-თქო` marks first-person speech that a second person is to pass on to a third. Both are separated from the word by a
hyphen, unlike `-ო` (Kvachadze §194):
- ✅ `ტყუილია-მეთქი` · ✅ `ვარ-მეთქი` · ✅ `მოიქეცი-თქო`

### -თქვა as a Particle Is an Error
The particle `-თქვა` counts as a mistake in the literary language. Separately, some dialects, such as Imeretian, use
`-თქვა` or `-თქო` where a speaker repeats their own words; the book corrects both to `-მეთქი` for literary Georgian,
so `-თქო` in that use is non-literary too (Kvachadze §194):
- ✅ `ვარ-მეთქი` · ❌ `ვარ-თქვა` · ✅ `მოველაპარაკები-მეთქი` · ❌ `მოველაპარაკები-თქო`

### Position of the Subordinate Clause in an Author's Tag
The place of the subordinate clause depends on where the author's complex sentence stands. If the author's sentence
follows the direct speech (or is set inside it), the subordinate clause comes after the main clause. If the author's
sentence comes before the direct speech, the subordinate clause comes first. The subordinate clause can also sit inside
the main clause, but then the main clause's predicate still closes the complex sentence. In such sentences the
subordinate clause is mostly an adverbial clause of time or an attributive clause (Kvachadze §196):
- ✅ `— როგორ მოგეწონა ეს ამბავი? — ჰკითხა არჩილმა დას, როცა ოთახში შევიდნენ.`
- ✅ `თავს რომ წაადგა, უთხრა: გიორგი!..`
