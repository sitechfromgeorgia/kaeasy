# Georgian verb forms: detailed rules

Sources: Peikrishvili, Georgian Morphology (4th ed., 2010).
Rules and views attributed to Kvachadze (1988) or Chikobava (1946) reflect the literary norm of their time.

Short rules are in `verbs.md`, `verb-classes.md` and `person-markers.md`. This file holds the form rules: preverbs, voice, version, contact, thematic markers, verbal noun and participles.

---

## 1. Preverbs

### The fifteen preverbs
- Modern Georgian has 15 preverbs. Nine are simple: მი, მო, ა, და, გა, ჩა, შე, წა, გადა. Six are compound: ამო, ჩამო, შემო, წამო, გამო, გადმო. A compound is a simple preverb plus მო (შე+მო gives შემო). Example: შემოვიდა, გადმოვიდა. (Peikrishvili §93)

### Preverb and aspect
- A preverb is the general means of expressing perfective aspect. As a rule a verb with a preverb is perfective and one without is imperfective: დავხატე is perfective, ვხატე imperfective. Perfective shows an action as finished or certain to finish; imperfective shows the process as unfinished. (Peikrishvili §94)
- An imperfective form can be present, future or past. A perfective form has only past and future. (Peikrishvili §94, §99)
- In verbs that have aspect: the present subseries (present, imperfect, present subjunctive) is imperfective, the future subseries (future, conditional, future subjunctive) is perfective, and series II and III have both kinds. This is a general rule with exceptions, see the next two rules. Example: ვწერ → დავწერ (future), ვწერე → დავწერე. (Peikrishvili §99, §104)
- Shanidze treats the preverb as the only means of forming the perfective in modern Georgian; რ. ჭიკაძე (footnote) gives it a modest role in aspect: it only sets the present and future screeves against each other. (Peikrishvili §94)

### A preverb does not always turn a present into a future
- Test: if adding a preverb moves a present form into the future, the verb is aspectual (წერს present, დაწერს future). If it cannot, the book calls the verb aspectless: ქრის, მიქრის, დაქრის are all present. The same can happen in the past: წერდა and დაწერდა are both past. (Peikrishvili §99, §100)
- One verb can be aspectless with one preverb and aspectual with another: მიარხევს, მიათრევს and დაათრევს are aspectless, but შეარხევს and წაათრევს are aspectual. (Peikrishvili §100)
- Aspectless verbs are usually intransitive, rarely transitive (ვიცი). The book names two groups: verbs that take a preverb only to show direction (მიდის, მიფრინავს, დაბზუის), and verbs it says take no preverb at all, a group of intransitives, mostly middle voice (უყვარს, ჰყავს, სძულს, არის), and static passives (გდია, ყრია, აფენია). (Peikrishvili §100)
- Some verbs that do carry a preverb can be imperfective or perfective depending on context: აღნიშნავს, განმარტავს, განიხილავს, აღწერს. (Peikrishvili §100)
- The term "aspectless" is disputed: a footnote reports the view that such verbs have one aspect and lack its partner. (Peikrishvili §100)

### The aorist does not always need a preverb
- Some verbs keep a remnant of the Old Georgian system, in which series II was perfective whether or not a preverb was present: თქვა, ნახა, ჰკითხა, სთხოვა, ბრძანა, უთხრა have no preverb but are understood as perfective, because they are in series II. A footnote reports another view (გ. მაჭავარიანი, shared by ზ. ჭუმბურიძე): these verbs express neither aspect, only a single momentary action. (Peikrishvili §99)
- Most verbs are different: without a preverb their aorist is the imperfective, showing the process as unfinished: ვწერე, ვხატე. (Peikrishvili §99)

### Some verbs form the future without a preverb
- Some verbs without a preverb stayed perfective, so their old present-group forms moved into the future group: they look like presents but mean the future (ვნახავ was once a present). That left the present empty, and the language filled it with new verbs built on the passive participle in -ულ or -ილ, so ვიყიდი is the future and ვყიდულობ the present. (Peikrishvili §155)
- The book does not give a -ულობ present for every such verb. This type also produced parallel presents beside existing ones. Examples: future ვნახავ, ვიყიდი, ვიშოვი, ვილოცავ; present ვყიდულობ, ვშოულობ, ვპოულობ, ვლოცულობ; parallel presents ვიღებ / ვღებულობ, ვიგებ / ვგებულობ. (Peikrishvili §155)

### Spelling the preverb
- Write the literary vowel of the preverb, not the dialect vowel. Dialects can change it (წე- for წა-, შა- for შე-); the book calls this unacceptable in the literary language: წავიდა, not წევიდა; შეიყრებიან, not შაიყრებიან. (Peikrishvili §96, point 1)
- Keep both vowels. When a verb is formed from a word that begins with ა and takes the preverb ა or a preverb ending in ა, two ა stand side by side: გაადვილდა, გაახლდა. When a verb begins with ე and takes the preverb შე, two ე stand side by side: შეეშინდა, შეეპარა. The book gives only these two cases. (Peikrishvili §96, points 2-3)
- In syllable division, neither the person marker nor the first consonant of the stem stays with the preverb: შე-სრულდა, და-ვხატე, not შეს-რულდა, დავ-ხატე. The page speaks of syllable division (დამარცვლა), not of line ends. (Peikrishvili §96, point 4)
- Never split a preverb. Write გან- whole and put the following ა after it, not between გა and ნ: განახორციელა, განათავისუფლა, განავითარა, not გაანხორციელა, გაანთავისუფლა, გაანვითარა. This is not about the compound preverbs (simple preverb plus მო). (Peikrishvili §96, point 5)
- In forms without a preverb, the final consonant of გან, აღ or წარ (ნ, ღ, რ) must not remain: ახორციელებს, ავითარებს, ადგენს, ზრდის, not ანხორციელებს, ანვითარებს, არდგენს, ღზრდის. (Peikrishvili §96, point 6)

## 2. Voice

### Three ways a dynamic passive is built
- Prefixal: ი- gives a one-person passive, mostly from primary verbs and rarely from nouns or adjectives (იზრდება ის). ე- usually gives a two-person passive (ეზრდება ის მას), with a few one-person exceptions today that once had two persons (დაეცა ის). (Peikrishvili §120)
- Suffixal: the marker is -დ-, mostly on verbs from nouns or adjectives, with a few primary verbs as exceptions (შენდება); these can be one-person (თეთრდება, წითლდება ის) or two-person (უწითლდება ის მას). (Peikrishvili §120)
- Unmarked: no voice marker, one-person or two-person, limited to a few old roots and no longer productive: დნება. (Peikrishvili §120)

### Doubled vowels and consonants in voice forms
- When an ი- passive takes the preverb მი-, two ი meet: მიიმალა, მიიპარება. (Peikrishvili §131, point 1)
- When an ე- passive takes the preverb შე-, two ე meet: შეეჩვია, შეეპარა. When that ე- passive is built from a word that itself begins with ე, three ე meet: ეჭვი → ეეჭვება → შეეეჭვა. (Peikrishvili §131, points 2-3)
- When a stem ends in the consonant დ and the passive suffix -დ- is added, two დ result: დიდდება, გაგულადდა. (Peikrishvili §131, point 4)

## 3. Version

### The objective vowel depends on the recipient's person
- The objective version says the direct object belongs to, or is meant for, the indirect object. Its marker is უ- only when that indirect object is 3rd person; with a 1st or 2nd person indirect object the marker is ი-. (Peikrishvili §134-135)
- The subjective version also uses ი-, so the two look alike in 1st and 2nd person. Two tests: (1) put the 1st or 2nd person form into the 3rd person: if ი- stays it is subjective, if უ- replaces it, it is objective. (2) A two-person transitive verb is subjective; a three-person transitive verb is objective. (Peikrishvili §134-135)
- Example: მიწერს (for me), გიწერს (for you), უწერს (for him); ვიწერ, იწერს (for oneself). (Peikrishvili §134-135)

### ი- and უ- that carry no beneficiary meaning
- In A. Shanidze's view, heavy use of the subjective version made some verbs lose their neutral forms, so an ი- form now does the neutral job (old პოვა, now იპოვა). The passage concludes that function outranks form, so such verbs count as neutral. Example: დაიწყო, წაიღო, გაიგო, იპოვა. (Peikrishvili §134)
- A transitive verb has no version in series III: the ი- and უ- of the first evidential (perfect) are functionless, because აკეთებს, იკეთებს and უკეთებს all give the one form გაუკეთებია. Example: დაუწერია, გაუკეთებია. (Peikrishvili §137, §161)
- On the traditional view, a verb with no contrasting version form is versionless. Such a verb can look like one version, but its ა-, ი- or უ- is functionless (credited to Shanidze). Example: აქვს, იცის, უყვარს. (Peikrishvili §138)
- The first perfect starts from the static passive present with objective version, without any version meaning here: დაუწერია, აუშენებია. (Peikrishvili §161)

## 4. Contact (causative)

- Mediated contact (the subject acts on the object through another person) is marked by a suffix attached to the series I theme. The book names three: -ინ for the great majority of verbs; -ევ, the old main formant, now on only one or two verbs; and the compound -ევინ (from ევ + ინ) for ი-theme verbs, all ამ-verbs and some ავ-verbs. (Peikrishvili §143, §143ა)
- Two people really act: the leader (organizer) and the actual executor. In the book's example the executor is შვილს; the page states no case rule for either person. Generally three persons are involved, so the form is built from a three-person transitive verb, with rare exceptions. Example: აკეთებინებს დედა შვილს საქმეს, აწერინებს, აჭმევს, ასმევს, აშლევინებს, ადგმევინებს, აკვლევინებს. (Peikrishvili §143, §143ა)
- Spell the causative suffix in full: write the whole formant (-ინ, -ევინ), do not drop its ნ. Example: დააწერინა, წააშლევინა, not დააწერია, წააშლევია. (Peikrishvili §144)
- In ავ-verbs the ვ of the theme marker must stay before -ინ (credited to G. Shalamberidze). The reason given, quoting A. Shanidze, is that the causative starts from the series I theme as seen in the infinitive, which has this ვ and loses only its final ა. Example: დაახატვინა, ალოკვინებს, ახატვინებს, not დაახატია. (Peikrishvili §144)

## 5. Thematic markers and series III

- In the first evidential (perfect, series III) a verb with the theme marker ავ or ამ keeps that marker: დაუხატავს, დაუბამს, not დაუხატია, დაუბია. The book says the modern literary norm allows only the forms with the marker. A footnote adds that the form in -ია is used in dialects and, under their influence, fairly often in written language too, and that Z. Chumburidze therefore allows it beside the -ავს form. (Peikrishvili §152, point 1, and footnote 3)
- A verb with the suffix ებ adds -ინ after the theme marker in the second evidential and in the third subjunctive (both series III). The forms without ნ, in -ია and -იოს, are wrong: გამეკეთებინა, გამეკეთებინოს, not გამეკეთებია, გამეკეთებიოს. (Peikrishvili §152, point 6)
- Where both exist, the long pluperfect and subjunctive with ებ are the literary norm. In Old Georgian, active verbs built the second pluperfect and the third subjunctive only on the passive aorist and second subjunctive. Modern Georgian sometimes also bases them on the first-perfect form with its thematic marker, which gives parallel short and long forms (short გაეკეთა beside long გაეკეთებინა). The page says the form with the marker ებ is today's literary norm. This does not make every verb long: the forms for the verb წერ are დამეწერა and დამეწეროს, with no ებ. Example: გაეკეთებინა, გაეკეთებინოს. (Peikrishvili §161)
- Perfect and pluperfect forms with the suffix ნ (the უყიდნია type) are not accepted in the literary language: ❌ უყიდნია, გაუტაცნია, მოუმატნია, ეყიდნა, მოემატნა. A. Shanidze's view: the ნ arose to separate active forms from similar passive ones (active უცხოვრებია, passive უცხოვრნია) and then spread by analogy from the passive into active verbs. A footnote gives other scholars' views of its origin. (Peikrishvili §161)
- Two-person dynamic passive verbs build the second pluperfect and the third subjunctive organically: in place of an auxiliary verb they take the extension ოდ, followed by the screeve marker. In these two screeves verbs with the suffix ევ lose the ვ in front of that ოდ: გაჰქცეოდა, გაჰქცეოდეს. The form გაჰქცევია has no ოდ, so its ვ stays. (Peikrishvili §162)

## 6. Stems that change

- Usually the plural is built from the singular stem (ვწერ, ვწერთ), but some verbs put a different stem in the plural. The switch concerns the subject (with intransitive verbs) or the direct object (possible only with transitive verbs). Example: ზის → სხედან, ვარდება → ცვივდება, ჩააგდო → ჩაყარა, მოკლა → დახოცა. (Peikrishvili §156)
- Some verbs lack certain screeves and fill the gap from a different stem; two, and sometimes three, stems take turns across a verb's forms. The book calls this a wider phenomenon than the stem change in number. Example: ვამბობ → ვიტყვი → ვთქვი, ვეუბნები → ვეტყვი → ვუთხარი, მაქვს → მექნება, ვხედავ → დავინახავ. (Peikrishvili §156)

## 7. Verbal noun (masdar)

- For the great majority of verbs, the masdar is made by adding the suffix ა to the present/future stem without any stem extension. To get that stem, take off the markers of person, number and screeve, the extension, and the affixes that form version and voice. The page calls this the norm for modern Georgian. Example: შენება (from აშენებს), მზადება (from ემზადება). (Peikrishvili §168)
- Before the masdar ა, the thematic ი is cut off. The thematic suffixes ავ and ამ lose only their vowel ა, so their ვ or მ stays in the masdar. Example: შლა (from შლის), ხატვა (from ხატავს), ბმა (from ა-ბ-ამ-ს). (Peikrishvili §168)
- Besides plain ა, the page names three further types. Some intransitive verbs show an infix ომ before the masdar ა (ჯდომა, სხდომა). In others an infix ოლ is inserted at the same point (ბრძოლა, წოლა). The third type inserts a suffix ნ before ა, especially in stems ending in ვ (თხოვნა, პოვნა). A footnote gives A. Chikobava's view that ომ and ოლ form the verb stem and do not form the masdar. The remnants of old masdar formation (ილ, ს-ა, სი-ილ, სი-ულ) are a separate group. (Peikrishvili §168)
- The masdar declines for case, but no ებ plural is formed from it. If it passes fully into the class of nouns and breaks its link with the verb, it does take ებ, and the page then counts such words as nouns: განცხადებები, კითხვები, სხდომები. (Peikrishvili §167)
- Whether the masdar has a plural at all is disputed: A. Shanidze is cited as saying it has no number, and I. Imnaishvili as objecting, with forms in -ანი. The author adds that such ნ plurals do occur, rarely and mostly in writing. (Peikrishvili §167)

## 8. Participles

- Active participle (the action of the verb's subject): built on the theme of the first series. Its formatives are მ-, მ-...-ელ and მა-...-ელ, of which მ- is the main one; in the page's examples a preverb stands before them. Example: დამწერი, მშენებელი, დამაარსებელი. (Peikrishvili §170)
- Some active participles were formed with მ-...-არ (ალ), but such words are now treated as nouns naming a profession (მხატვარი, მწერალი). A few others are formed in their own way, such as მსაჯული and მეტყველი. (Peikrishvili §170)
- The active participle shows its thematic marker everywhere except in verbs with the marker ი. If a verb with ავ or ამ takes the suffix ელ in the participle, that marker loses its vowel ა. Example: დამბმელი (from ა-ბ-ამ-ს), მშლელი (from შლის), მკვლელი (from კლავს). (Peikrishvili §170)
- Future passive participle: the affix სა- or the pair სა-...-ელ, with the preverb before სა-. Example: დასაწერი, გასატეხი, დასაშლელი. (Peikrishvili §171)
- Negative passive participle: the prefix უ- together with the suffix ელ, or the prefix უ- alone, with the preverb in front of უ-. Among medial-voice participles, the page gives negative forms only for the stem ვალ. Example: დაუწერელი, გაუთლელი, დაუხატავი. (Peikrishvili §171)
- Medial-voice participles are less settled than active and passive ones (A. Shanidze): some verbs have none, verbs of one type can differ, and one root can have two forms. (Peikrishvili §172)
- Mediopassive participle: mainly the pair მ-...-არე, built on the masdar: დგას, then დგომა, then მდგომარე. Example: მდგომარე, მჯდომარე, მწოლარე. (Peikrishvili §172)
- Mediactive participle: four formatives, მ- alone, მო-...-ე, მ-...-არ (also ალ) and მ-...-არე (also ალე); the page gives no rule for which verb takes which, and says they are formed differently from mediopassive ones. Example: მცურავი, მოთამაშე, მყივარი, მტირალი, მქუხარე. (Peikrishvili §172)
